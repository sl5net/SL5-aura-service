# The License explained in German

The **Apache License, Version 2.0** is definitely **Free Software** and also **Open Source Software**.

Here are the details of why this is the case and what it means for you:

### 1. Official Recognition
*   **FSF (Free Software Foundation):** It classifies the Apache 2.0 as a license for 'Free Software.' However, it is **not compatible with GPL Version 2**, but compatible with **GPL Version 3**.
*   **OSI (Open Source Initiative):** It recognizes the Apache 2.0 as one of the most popular and standardized open-source licenses.

### 2. what to do with it? (Freedoms)
Under the Apache 2.0 license, everyone (including companies) can:
*   **Use the software for any purpose:** Commercial, private, internal – everything allowed.
*   **Change the code:** You can adjust the tool as you wish.
*   **Copy and distribute the software:** You may share it.
*   **Even sell:** You may sell software based on Apache code.

### 3. The Special Feature: "Permissive" (Liberal)
Unlike the GPL (General Public License), the Apache License **does not have a 'copyleft' effect**.
*   That means: If you take Aura (under Apache 2.0) and modify it, you **do not** necessarily have to release your new code as open source again. You can also incorporate it into a closed, commercial product.

### 4. A great advantage: the patent clause
The Apache 2.0 license is very popular with companies (often more popular than MIT or BSD) because it includes an explicit patent grant.
*   Anyone who contributes code to the project automatically grants users a license for all patents they hold on that code.
*   This protects users from "patent trolls". If someone sues you for patent infringement who worked on the project himself, he automatically loses his rights to the software.

### Summary:
Under **Apache 2.0**, signal professionalism:

1.  ** Legal certainty:** Especially for large companies (such as in automotive or finance) this is the "safest" license.
2.  **Flexibility:** They can easily integrate your tool into their internal toolchains without having to fear the copyleft effect.

**Conclusion:** It is FOSS (Free and Open Source Software) in the best sense. It is “free” both in the sense of “free” and in the sense of “freedom.”
